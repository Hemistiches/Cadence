The name

In music, a cadence is the rhythm that carries a phrase and leads it to its resolution.

For a company without an in-house legal department, it is the legal time it actually needs, no more and no less: two, four or eight days a month from the firm, or twelve to sixteen days a month for the duration of a transition.

The test measures it from concrete situations. It never asks you to choose a number of days yourself.

How should your legal function be organised?

Ten questions at most, framed as concrete situations. The test adapts to your answers and never asks for documents or case details.

Two modes of intervention

One counsel for the whole of the company’s legal life: carrying the core work, bringing in the expertise required, keeping the tempo. From one mode to the other, only the organisation, the volume and the duration change.

From the firm

Outsourced general counsel

The work is carried out from my office, through remote exchanges, with occasional visits to your premises agreed in advance.

It keeps your legal priorities on track within a reserved monthly volume of 14, 28 or 56 hours. It is neither an unlimited on-call service nor a weekly presence at your offices.

  • 2, 4 or 8 days per month
  • Company law covered by the volume: corporate housekeeping, registered office, share transfers, capital; external costs extra
  • Initial term of six months, then continuation with one month’s notice
  • Monthly retainer and statement of work

To take over the function

Interim general counsel

The engagement takes over the legal function temporarily, on an agreed schedule: absence, departure, vacant position or a substantial temporary reinforcement. It is carried out remotely where possible, and on your premises when the engagement requires it: meetings, committees, key moments.

It requires 12 to 16 days per month. Days on site are included in the fees; any travel and accommodation costs are separate, estimated and agreed in advance.

  • 12 to 16 days per month
  • The whole function, company law included; external costs extra
  • Day rate depending on the agreed initial term
  • Schedule and organisation set in the engagement letter

Frequently asked questions

What happens if I end a plan before six months?

You may end the engagement at any time, with one month’s notice. As the plan rate is linked to an initial term of six months, early termination triggers the published rate for the period actually completed: that of commitments of less than 3 months, or of 3 to less than 6 months. The difference from the sums already paid is invoiced; no future monthly instalment is due.

Examples, Essentiel plan:

  • Ended after one month: “less than 3 months” rate, €1,800 excl. VAT instead of €1,500, i.e. a €300 excl. VAT top-up.
  • Ended after four months: “3 to less than 6 months” rate, 4 × €1,650 = €6,600 excl. VAT instead of €6,000, i.e. a €600 excl. VAT top-up.

Additional hours agreed and already invoiced are not affected. This rule does not apply if the termination results from a failure on the firm’s part or is at its initiative. It is set out in the engagement letter, which alone makes it applicable.

And after the first six months?

The engagement continues at the same rate, for an indefinite term, with one month’s notice on either side. You may reduce the volume at the end of the six months, subject to notice, and thereafter at any time with the same notice; increasing it requires written agreement. There is no automatic change of plan, and a new initial term only exists if you expressly accept it.

How are hours counted and carried over?

All work done for you uses up the month’s volume. Up to three unused hours roll over to the following month, are used first and expire at the end of that month. If a request within the agreed scope and timetable could not be handled by the firm, the corresponding hours are not lost: they are carried over to the following month without cap or, at your choice, deducted from the next retainer.

What if I need more hours in a given month?

I send you an estimate; the additional work is only done with your agreement, at €150 excl. VAT per hour and subject to my availability. If overruns become regular, I propose a better-suited volume.

Does the interim mode work the same way?

No. The interim mode is based on a day rate set according to the agreed initial term, for 12 to 16 days per month. The three-hour carry-over does not apply. If the engagement ends early, with one month’s notice, the days worked are charged at the day rate for the period actually completed; no future day not worked is charged. A start date is agreed in light of my existing commitments: I tell you the possible date in our first conversation.

Can you work on our premises under a plan?

Yes, occasionally and on dates agreed in advance. Time spent on site uses up the volume; travel and costs are agreed before the visit. If a regular presence is essential, the test will direct you to a personalised proposal.

Is the test result binding?

No. It is a personalised estimate based solely on your answers. It is neither legal advice nor a contractual offer: scope, volume and duration are confirmed in a conversation, then in a signed engagement letter.

Do you use artificial intelligence?

Yes, as a tool assisting legal work: research, first drafts, proofreading. The analysis, the choices and the responsibility remain the lawyer’s, and every document is reviewed before it reaches you. No confidential or sensitive information is submitted to an artificial intelligence tool without sufficient confidentiality safeguards; you may ask that no such tool be used on your matter. The time saved shows in response times and in prices fixed in advance.
